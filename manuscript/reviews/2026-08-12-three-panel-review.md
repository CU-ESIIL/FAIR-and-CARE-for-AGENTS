# Three-panel review of the CARE scope revision

**Date:** 2026-08-12  
**Artifact reviewed:** `manuscript/fair_care_agentic_science_v2.md`, first CARE-scope revision  
**Review type:** Three independent simulated agent panels  
**Status:** Editorial input only; not human peer review, Indigenous governance review, community approval, or endorsement of the manuscript's interpretation of CARE

## Review question

Does the manuscript responsibly propose benefit, authority, responsibility, and ethics as considerations for every scientific workflow while preserving the origins, continuing purpose, and context-specific obligations of the CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance?

## Panel 1 — Indigenous Data Governance and research ethics

### Principal findings

1. The draft sometimes shifted from presenting an author-proposed extension to declaring CARE itself universal. That wording could imply authority to redefine an Indigenous-led framework.
2. The four workflow questions are an entry point, not an equivalent restatement of the substantive CARE Principles.
3. Indigenous governance cannot be treated as an extra layer added after a generic workflow is designed; it may constitute, reshape, supersede, or prohibit the workflow.
4. Licenses, access, institutional roles, technical control, and legitimate authority are not interchangeable.
5. A single responsible human cannot replace collective, institutional, or rights-holder responsibilities.
6. Project self-assessment cannot establish Collective Benefit, legitimate authority, or ethical legitimacy on behalf of affected groups.
7. Ethics must include cultural, political, social, spiritual, economic, labor, ecological, and intergenerational harms—not only scientifically plausible technical failures.
8. Publication still requires Indigenous Data Governance review. This panel supplies no such approval.

### Editorial disposition

Accepted. The manuscript now consistently distinguishes the original CARE Principles from the authors' proposed **CARE-informed questions**. It states that the questions are not compliance, certification, restatement, or endorsed expansion; broadens the possible Indigenous scope; treats contextual authority as potentially determinative; distinguishes permissions from legitimate authority; assigns operational, institutional, governing, scientific-review, and release roles separately; expands harm analysis; and retains Indigenous Data Governance review as a release blocker.

## Panel 2 — Ecology and environmental science

### Principal findings

1. The Indigenous CARE framework and the proposed general entry screen need stable, distinct terminology.
2. The central thesis should remain that FAIR and CARE improve science for people and must be designed into agentic workflows, rather than allowing universalization to become a competing thesis.
3. The normative extension needs broader evidence from Indigenous Data Sovereignty, environmental justice, responsible research, and agent-reliability literature.
4. Ecological relevance was too thin for an *Ecology* audience.
5. Authority needs to distinguish legal permission, institutional authorization, scientific competence, social legitimacy, consent, and collective authority.
6. The eight rules are operational interpretations, not proof of FAIR or CARE compliance.
7. Collective Benefit must preserve distributional questions rather than become generic beneficiary analysis.
8. Implementation should be proportional to consequence and acknowledge institutional support that small laboratories may need.

### Editorial disposition

Mostly accepted. The abstract and conclusion retain the human-science/agent-workflow thesis. The terminology is stabilized around **CARE** for the original principles and **CARE-informed questions** for the proposed general screen. A habitat-assessment example now links the framework to remote sensing, sensitive biodiversity observations, and governed knowledge. Authority, benefit distribution, operational-interpretation limits, proportionality, and shared institutional services are clearer. Four visible citation TODOs now record the evidence still needed; the manuscript does not present those arguments as fully substantiated.

## Panel 3 — Agentic systems, reproducibility, and technical editing

### Principal findings

1. `Goal → Instructions → Test → Record` did not directly encode the four proposed governance questions.
2. “Every workflow” conflicted with the heavier requirements for “consequential work.”
3. Instructions to attempt prohibited data movement or harmful actions could cause the exact harm being tested.
4. Agent, workflow, consequential work, authority, and accountable owner needed operational distinctions.
5. Responsibility required separate operational, institutional, governing, scientific-review, and release roles.
6. Authority needed scope, duration, conflict, revision, and revocation conditions.
7. Refusal needed to be distinguished from scientific, computational, and capability failure.
8. FAIR supports findability and reuse but does not establish scientific correctness.

### Editorial disposition

Accepted. Benefit and burden now appear in Goal; authority and prohibited actions in Instructions; benefit, harm, boundary, refusal, and stop criteria in Test; and accountable roles, approval, correction, and minimized provenance in Record. Every workflow receives a lightweight screen; defined consequential triggers invoke the full specification. Governance tests must use synthetic fixtures, mocks, or isolated pre-authorized environments. The manuscript distinguishes outcome classes and no longer treats FAIR as a correctness test.

## Unresolved work after the second edit

- Obtain substantive review from Indigenous Data Governance scholars or the appropriate Indigenous governance authorities before publication.
- Vet and add evidence on coding-agent reliability, tool-mediated risks, provenance, and boundary following.
- Vet and add Indigenous-led scholarship and, where appropriate and authorized, nation- or community-specific governance protocols.
- Vet and add literature supporting the ecological governance, environmental-justice, and sensitive-biodiversity example.
- Complete the pre-existing evidence gap on machine-actionable environmental repository metadata.
- Have the responsible author decide which new material to retain for the journal-targeted draft and approve all scientific and normative claims.

## Editorial outcome

The second edit adopts the panels' shared recommendation: the manuscript does **not** claim to universalize or redefine CARE. It proposes that every workflow undergo a lightweight screen using four CARE-informed questions, with fuller requirements for consequential work. The original CARE Principles retain their Indigenous purpose, and legitimate context-specific authority may determine, reshape, or prohibit a workflow.
